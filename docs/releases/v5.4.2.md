# OpenInterviewer 5.4.2

Voice input now tells participants what is happening and never leaves them
talking into silence. This mainly affects on-device voice input (added in
5.4.0), where Chrome's recognizer could record without returning any text or
error.

## What changed

- **Ready.** After Chrome downloads its speech pack, the participant sees
  "Voice input is ready. Press the microphone to speak." The microphone still
  never opens on its own.
- **Starting.** "Starting the microphone…" shows until the browser is actually
  capturing sound. "Listening…" then means it really is.
- **Live text.** Words appear under the answer box as they are recognized. They
  stay on the participant's screen and are not saved or sent.
- **Unfinished speech is kept.** If the browser recognizes speech but never
  marks it finished, that text goes into the answer box when the participant
  stops. As before, nothing is sent until the participant presses Send.
- **No more silent failures.** If no text was recognized, or the browser stopped
  listening unexpectedly, the participant is told and can try again or type.
  A stuck recognizer is released after a few seconds, so Send never stays
  disabled.
- **Chinese and Japanese.** Dictated text is no longer joined with spaces.
- The new messages are in all six interview languages.

On-device voice input still runs only in the browser on the participant's
computer. Nothing about where speech is processed has changed.

## Testing status

The new voice behavior passes automated tests, which simulate the browser's
speech recognizer. It has **not yet been tested by hand with real speech**.

- Chrome's on-device recognizer can't be driven by automated tests.
- Before this release, on-device voice input worked by hand in English. In
  Chinese it recorded but returned no text, and the cause, inside Chrome, is
  not yet known.
- This release makes that failure visible to participants and keeps any partly
  recognized text. Whether it makes Chinese work is untested.
- Until on-device voice input is confirmed for a study's language, treat it as
  experimental there and tell participants they can type their answers.

This release also includes 5.4.1 (Chinese and Japanese dashes and ellipses),
which had no separate release.

## Upgrade and rollback

A code-only release, with no schema, configuration or binding changes. Rolling
back to 5.4.1 or 5.4.0 is safe.
